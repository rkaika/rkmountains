Interpretation notes
Baseline

NWS first

Use the NWS hourly data here as the official, human-edited baseline. For raw model maps, compare Windy layers at the same time, layer, and altitude.

Close-in

HRRR inside 48 hours

For a 1 am start, HRRR is usually the lead model for shower timing, wind shifts, and cloud detail once you are within about two days.

Cross-check

Globals + mesoscale

ECMWF is usually most accurate for 3–7 day synoptic timing; GEM often sees Pacific systems first; ICON and GFS add independent votes. If HRRR/NAM and the globals agree, confidence is high — if they diverge close-in, trust the mesoscale model.

Confidence

Spread = uncertainty

The Model Spread tile shows wind agreement across the six models. Tight (<8 mph) = high confidence; wide (≥15 mph) means check back closer to climb day before committing.

Coordinates are precomputed route references, not official navigation data. NWS grid terrain is smoothed, so summit ridges can be colder, windier, or cloudier than the forecast grid cell.
