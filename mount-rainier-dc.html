Mount Rainier south-side route overview

Mount Rainier DC / Ingraham Direct

The standard south-side climb: Paradise to Camp Muir on the Muir Snowfield, then over Cathedral Gap to Ingraham Flats, and up either the Disappointment Cleaver or, early season, the Ingraham Direct to Columbia Crest. Roughly 16 miles round trip and 9,000 feet of gain, most of it above 10,000 feet on active glaciers.

The climbing route changes week to week as crevasses open and ladders and fixed lines move. Numbers here are planning context — re-verify the current route, permits, and weather before departure.

01 / Goal

Elevation profile and major waypoints

Paradise to Camp Muir is a long snowfield approach that may be done in trail crampons. Above Muir, the climb is roped glacier terrain with route-dependent hazards. Treat the two halves as different efforts and budget energy accordingly.

Primary objective: return the full team to Paradise safely. Build the plan around turnaround criteria, not the summit.
Waypoint 1
Paradise

5,400 ft. Paved parking, ranger station, restrooms. Summer mornings may require a timed-entry reservation in addition to the park entrance fee.

Waypoint 2
Camp Muir

10,188 ft. About 4.5 miles and 4,800 ft above Paradise via the Muir Snowfield. Public shelter (first come), tent sites, pit toilets, no running water — melt snow.

Waypoint 3
Columbia Crest

14,411 ft. Another ~4,200 ft above Muir across the Cowlitz and Ingraham Glaciers. Summit day typically starts between midnight and 2 AM.

02 / Route Choice

Ingraham Direct vs Disappointment Cleaver

Both variations share Paradise, Camp Muir, and Ingraham Flats. They split above the Flats. Current conditions determine which variation is used, so check the climbing blog and ask the rangers when you register.

Disappointment Cleaver (DC)

The standard line from roughly mid-June onward. Traverse from the Flats onto the rock rib of the Cleaver, climb it to ~12,300 ft, then follow the guided trough up the upper mountain to the crater rim.

Watch for: rockfall on and below the Cleaver, crowding at fixed-line sections, and a dusty loose-rock climb late season when snow melts off. Space out, move efficiently, and minimize stops in the hazard zone.

Ingraham Direct (ID)

The early-season line, typically May into June. From the Flats it climbs the Ingraham Glacier directly toward the crater rim; it is shorter and often faster than the DC while cold and well-bridged.

Watch for: serac and icefall hazard from the Ingraham Icefall overhead, crevasse bridges that weaken as the season warms, and a route that can close quickly in warm weather. When ID closes, traffic moves to the DC.

03 / Route Sequence

Route sequence from Paradise to Columbia Crest

Paradise to Muir is navigation and pacing. Muir to the summit is rope management, hazard timing, and altitude. Plan the climb by segment rather than as one continuous day.

1

Paradise, 5,400 ft

Register or confirm your permit at the ranger station, check the posted route conditions, and start organized. The Skyline Trail leaves from behind the visitor center.

2

Skyline Trail to Pebble Creek, 7,200 ft

Maintained trail past Panorama Point. Settle into the intended pace here; arriving at Pebble Creek with margin improves the snowfield climb.

3

Muir Snowfield, 7,200–10,188 ft

Three thousand feet of sustained snow travel. In a whiteout this terrain is difficult to navigate; carry bearings or a GPS track for the descent before you need them.

4

Camp Muir, 10,188 ft

Shelter is first-come; most parties tent. Melt snow, rig the rope and harnesses before dark, and prepare for a near-midnight start.

5

Cowlitz Glacier and Cathedral Gap

Roped from camp. Cross the Cowlitz, climb the rocky switchbacks of the Gap, and reach the Ingraham. Crampons on rock — deliberate feet, short spacing.

6

Ingraham Flats, 11,100 ft

High camp for some parties and the route split. ID climbs straight up the glacier; the DC traverses right under the icefall runout to the Cleaver's toe. Minimize time in that stretch.

7

The Cleaver or the Direct, to ~12,300 ft

On the DC: rock and snow, rockfall hazard, helmets on, and minimal stops. Most parties clear it before dawn. On the ID: crevasse bridges and ladders, with serac hazard overhead.

8

Upper mountain to the crater rim

Long switchbacks at altitude. Wind is usually strongest here. Use pressure breathing and rest steps; this section is a common turnaround point.

9

Crater rim and Columbia Crest, 14,411 ft

The true summit is across the crater, 15–20 minutes farther. If time and conditions allow, tag Columbia Crest, then start down before the Cleaver warms.

04 / Summit-Day Timing

Typical summit-day schedule and turnaround

Use an early start to manage objective hazards: cross the icefall runout and the Cleaver while they are frozen, aim for a mid-morning summit, and clear the Cleaver again before afternoon warming.

Turn around if you are not at the top of the Cleaver by your agreed time, or if the route is warming quickly on descent. Descending before afternoon warming is part of the plan.

Weather checks by elevation

06 / Permits & Getting There

Permits, reservations, and access

Climbing above 10,000 ft or on glaciers requires both the annual Climbing Cost Recovery Fee and a climbing permit, and staying at Camp Muir or Ingraham Flats requires a wilderness permit for those camps. Summer mornings may also need a Paradise timed-entry reservation. Verify all of it on the NPS pages below — quotas and rules change.

  • Climbing fee + permit: pay the annual climbing fee, then register for the climb itself — in person at the Paradise ranger station or as directed on the NPS climbing page.
  • Camp quota: Camp Muir and Ingraham Flats have nightly limits; weekend spots go early. Reserve the wilderness permit well ahead.
  • Timed entry: check whether the Paradise corridor requires a vehicle reservation for your dates and entry time.
  • Drive: roughly 2.5 hours from Seattle to Paradise via the Nisqually entrance. Expect no useful cell signal past Longmire.
  • Blue bags: required above the camps — pack out human waste. Pick them up when you register.

07 / Trip Plans

Trip plan options

Classic two-day

Day 1Paradise to Camp Muir by early afternoon. Rig gear, eat, sleep by 19:00.
Day 2Alpine start, summit, descend all the way to Paradise.

Three-day with skills

Day 1Paradise to Camp Muir.
Day 2Rest, snow and crevasse-rescue practice, early sleep.
Day 3Summit and descend to Paradise.

High-camp variation

Day 1Paradise to Camp Muir.
Day 2Move roped to Ingraham Flats high camp — shortens summit day by ~1.5 h.
Day 3Summit from the Flats and descend to Paradise.

A Paradise-to-Camp-Muir day trip a few weeks earlier is a useful rehearsal for pacing, gear, and snowfield navigation without glacier travel.

08 / Preparation

Altitude, fitness, and rope skills

Rainier is about 4,000 ft higher than Baker, and most parties feel the altitude above the Cleaver. Fitness, packing discipline, rope skills, and an acclimatization plan all matter here.

  1. Train with weighted snowfield days; the Muir Snowfield with a full pack is a useful baseline.
  2. Practice crevasse rescue and running belays with your actual rope team until roles are clear.
  3. Taper the final week and arrive rested; sleep at Muir is often limited.
  4. Hydrate on Day 1 and keep eating on summit day even when altitude reduces appetite.
  5. Know the early signs of AMS and HACE/HAPE, and agree that symptoms override the summit objective.
  6. Practice the full midnight routine in the dark: boots, crampons, harness, rope-up, in wind.
  7. Carry bearings or a GPS track for the Muir Snowfield descent before you need them.
  8. Helmet on from Cathedral Gap until you are back below it.
  9. Agree splits and the turnaround time the night before, out loud, as a team.
  10. Pack blue bags, sunscreen, and glacier glasses; sunburn and snow blindness are common preventable problems.
09 / Descent

Descent plan and late-day hazards

Descent
Two stages

Summit to Muir is still roped glacier work on tired legs, and accidents often happen on descent. Muir to Paradise is a long snowfield descent; late-day whiteouts are common.

Timing
Afternoon hazards

Soft snow, wet slides, and rockfall increase through the afternoon. Clear the Cleaver early; on the snowfield, glissade only where you can see the runout and never in crampons.

After
Drive or stay

You will have been awake ~20 hours. Plan a driver swap, a nap at Paradise, or a night nearby; include post-climb travel in the plan.
