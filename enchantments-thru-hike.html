Enchantments Thru-Hike

The day traverse from Stuart Lake trailhead over Aasgard Pass, across the granite basin of the core, and down the Snow Lakes trail to Icicle Creek Road. About 18 to 19 miles one way, almost all of it on rock.

A long, rough day with one committing decision at Colchuck Lake. Numbers here are planning context. Re-verify permits, road status, trip reports, and weather before you go.

01 / Route

Elevation profile and major waypoints

The traverse has three very different parts: a forest trail to Colchuck Lake, a steep cairned climb of Aasgard Pass, and a long descent past a dozen lakes to a hot, dry trailhead. Budget effort for each separately. Most parties are surprised by how much the descent costs.

Primary objective: reach Snow Lakes trailhead with the whole party in daylight. Build the day around the Colchuck Lake decision and the pace gates.
Waypoint 1
Stuart Lake TH

3,400 ft. End of Eightmile Road. Self-issue day permit box, toilet, small lot that fills before dawn on summer weekends.

Waypoint 2
Colchuck Lake

5,570 ft, about 4 miles in. Last comfortable place to turn around. Aasgard rises 2,200 ft directly above the far shore.

Waypoint 3
Aasgard Pass

7,841 ft, about 5.3 miles in. The route's high point and the gateway to the upper core. From here the only easy exit is forward.

Waypoint 4
Snow Lakes TH

1,300 ft, 18 to 19 miles. On Icicle Creek Road, 4.5 miles from Leavenworth. Toilet, large lot, no water.

02 / Direction

West to east, or the alternatives

Almost everyone goes west to east: start high at Stuart Lake trailhead, climb Aasgard, and descend the gradual Snow Lakes trail. The reverse saves nothing and puts the loose, steep part of the day on tired legs, facing downhill.

West to east (this guide)

Stuart Lake TH to Snow Lakes TH. About 2,000 ft less climbing than the reverse, Aasgard is climbed rather than descended, and the core is crossed in morning light. The cost is a 7,000 ft descent at the end of the day.

East to west

Snow Lakes TH to Stuart Lake TH. A long, gradual 6,500 ft climb to the core, then a descent of Aasgard on loose rock. Some parties choose it for shuttle reasons. Descending Aasgard tired is where many injuries happen; treat it as the harder option.

Not a thru-hike day

Colchuck Lake out and back (about 8 miles) is the right call when the pass has snow, weather is marginal, or the party is slow at the lake. Snow Lakes to Lake Viviane out and back is a long but non-committing way to see the lower core. An overnight in the core requires a lottery permit.

03 / Route Sequence

Stuart Lake trailhead to Snow Lakes trailhead

Below Colchuck Lake the route is a maintained trail. From the lake to Lake Viviane it is cairned travel over boulders and granite slab, with some hands-on sections. Below Viviane it is trail again, mostly rock and roots, then sand and switchbacks.

1

Stuart Lake trailhead, 3,400 ft

Mile 0

Fill out the day permit at the box, display your parking pass, and start with headlamps for an alpine start. Good trail up Mountaineer Creek.

2

Stuart Lake and Colchuck junction, 4,450 ft

Mile 2.5

Signed junction. Left for Colchuck Lake. The trail steepens through boulders and switchbacks; the lake appears suddenly.

3

Colchuck Lake, 5,570 ft

Mile 4.0

The decision point. Eat, fill water, look at the pass, and check the party. The trail follows the west and south shore through a boulder field marked by cairns rather than tread; hitting it at first light saves time and confusion.

4

Aasgard Pass, 7,841 ft

Mile 5.3, about 2,200 ft in 0.8 miles

Climb to the left of the creek and waterfall, following cairns up ledges and scree. Loose rock throughout; do not stack the party directly above one another. The last easy water is near the base. Budget 2 to 2.5 hours for a moderate party.

5

Upper core: Isolation, Tranquil, Inspiration, Perfection

Miles 5.3 to 7.5, roughly 7,800 to 7,100 ft

Granite slab and tarns with the trail marked by cairns. Route-finding is vague where tread disappears onto rock, especially between Inspiration and Perfection. Filter water at Isolation or Perfection.

6

Lower core: Sprite, Leprechaun, Lake Viviane, 6,785 ft

Mile 9

Gentler walking past larches and Prusik Peak to Viviane, the last lake in the basin. This is the natural lunch stop and the point at which the day is roughly half done in time, not in effort.

7

Viviane to Upper Snow Lake, 5,415 ft

Mile 10.3, about 1,400 ft down

Steep polished slabs beside Snow Creek, with a short rebar-assisted step. Stow poles and use hands. Slower is correct here.

8

Snow Lakes dam and Nada Lake, 4,900 ft

Miles 11 to 12.5

Cross the dam between the two Snow Lakes; water can flow over it early in the season. Fill every bottle here or at Nada Lake. The trail below is largely dry for the remaining 5 to 6 miles.

9

Snow Lakes trailhead, 1,300 ft

Mile 18 to 19

Long sun-exposed switchbacks down to Icicle Creek Road. Near the very bottom, stay on the trail with a left turn toward the parking lot rather than following the obvious road into a residential area.

04 / Timing

A one-day schedule and its decision gates

Fit parties finish in 12 to 14 hours; 15 to 18 is common. Averages of 1.2 to 1.4 mph including breaks are normal because the terrain is rough. Time Aasgard for daylight, and set the gates below aloud at the trailhead so nobody has to argue about them at 7,800 feet.

Behind at Colchuck Lake

Thirty minutes down at the lake is recoverable. Make it up by moving through the core more briskly. Keep the break before the pass. An hour down, or a party member struggling on the approach trail, means the out-and-back is the day.

Not on Aasgard by the agreed time

This is the last inexpensive turnaround. Going back from the pass to Stuart Lake trailhead is about 5 miles. Past the pass, bailing means completing the traverse anyway, tired and in the dark. Set this gate around 10:30 for a 03:00 start.

Snow on the Aasgard headwall

Intermittent patches are what this plan assumes. Continuous snow with a runout into rock is a mountaineering route rather than a hike. On that day, make Colchuck Lake the objective.

Not at Lake Viviane by early afternoon

You will finish by headlamp. That is manageable on the Snow Lakes trail, but confirm every person has a real headlamp with fresh batteries and nobody is counting on a phone.

Once you are over the pass, the only exit is forward. The weather and party decision is made at Colchuck Lake, not in the core.
05 / Weather

Weather checks and what to watch

The core sits between 6,800 and 7,800 feet on the east slope of the Cascades. Watch for afternoon thunderstorms in summer, early or late-season snow that can arrive in a day, heat on the sun-exposed descent, and smoke from late-summer fires. Compare the pass forecast against the trailheads, and read the trend rather than a single number.

Thunderstorms

Summer convection builds over the crest in the afternoon. The core and the pass are exposed granite with nowhere to hide. If the forecast shows a thunderstorm signal, the plan is to be off the pass and out of the upper basin by early afternoon, or to make it a Colchuck day.

Snow arrives fast

In mid-September 2026 a single storm put one to three feet of snow on the pass and core. A day-hiking couple without overnight gear was stranded in the core, helicopters were turned back by weather, and one of them died of exposure before ground rescuers arrived. Pack for a night out, and treat any forecast precipitation at the pass as a reason to reconsider.

Heat and sun

The Snow Lakes trail below Nada Lake faces south and east with little shade, and it comes at the end of the day. In July and August the trailhead is often over 90°F. Fill every bottle at the dam or Nada Lake; the last miles are dry.

06 / Permits & Getting There

Permits, parking, roads, and the car shuttle

A day traverse needs no lottery permit: fill out the free self-issued day-use permit at the trailhead and display a Northwest Forest Pass or America the Beautiful pass on the car. Camping anywhere in the permit area from mid-May through October requires an overnight permit from the annual or daily lottery on Recreation.gov. Verify the current rules on the Forest Service page; they change.

  • Group size: eight people maximum, including parties travelling together on separate permits. No dogs anywhere in the permit area.
  • Roads: Snow Lakes trailhead is about 4.5 miles up Icicle Creek Road from Leavenworth. Stuart Lake trailhead is another 4 miles up Icicle, then about 4 miles of rough gravel on Eightmile Road (FR 7601). Both roads are heavily potholed in most years; a sedan is fine if driven slowly. Budget about 30 minutes between the two trailheads.
  • Parking: the Stuart Lake lot is small and fills before dawn on summer weekends; overflow along the road is restricted, so arrive early or plan to walk from the overflow. Snow Lakes has a larger lot that also fills on weekends.
  • Shuttle: leave one car at Snow Lakes and drive everyone to Stuart Lake, or park at Snow Lakes and book a commercial shuttle from Leavenworth to Stuart Lake ahead of time. Hitching back up Icicle Creek Road at the end of the day works but adds an hour or more to a long day.
  • Fire restrictions: stage 1 restrictions banning open fires are typical from early summer; there is no reason to carry stove or fire gear on a day traverse.
  • Cell service: none on the route and unreliable at the trailheads. A satellite messenger is the emergency plan, not a backup. Leave the route, both trailheads, vehicle details, and a "call for help by" time with someone at home.

07 / Hazards & Preparation

Where this route hurts people, and how to prepare

Injuries on the traverse are rarely dramatic. They come from loose rock on Aasgard, wrong turns that add miles, wet slab below Viviane, exhaustion and cramping on the final descent, and weather that changes faster than a day-hiking party expects.

  1. Aasgard route. Stay to the climber's left of the creek and waterfall on the way up. The right-hand side leads onto steep, wet slab where people have died. If you find yourself on slick rock beside running water, you are off route.
  2. Loose rock. Aasgard is scree and unstable boulders for 2,200 feet. Spread the party laterally or move in pods with a gap, and call any rock you knock loose.
  3. Buried cairns. Even a few inches of new snow hides the route across the pass and the core. Carry the GPS track offline on more than one phone and check it at every gap between cairns.
  4. Snow patches. Old snow over talus is undercut by melt; probe with a pole before weighting anything that spans boulders. Prefer bare rock over snow even when the rock is steeper.
  5. Daylight. Time the pass for first light rather than headlamps, and know civil dusk for your date. Everyone carries a headlamp and spare batteries.
  6. Water. Plentiful from Colchuck Lake through Nada Lake; nearly absent below it. Carry 2.5 to 3 liters of capacity and a filter per two people, and leave Nada full.
  7. Fuel and salt. Cramps on the last descent are usually hours-old sodium and calorie debt. Eat on a schedule, use electrolytes, and carry something salty for the tenth hour.
  8. Feet. Almost the whole route is rock. Tape known hot spots before the pass, carry Leukotape, and bring dry socks if snow is reported.
  9. Mountain goats. Habituated to human urine and salt. Pee on bare rock well away from trail and camps, keep 50 yards, and never feed them or let them lick packs.
  10. Night out. Carry enough insulation and a shell to survive an unplanned night at 7,000 feet. It is the difference between an uncomfortable story and the alternative.
08 / Gear

Day-traverse kit

Expect a temperature spread of 30 to 40 degrees across the day: cold and dark at the start, sun at the pass, hot at the end. Pack light enough to move, but not so light that an unplanned night is dangerous.

Carry every time

  • Headlamp and spare batteries (not a phone light)
  • Trekking poles for 7,000 ft of descent
  • 2.5 to 3 L water capacity and a filter
  • Offline GPS track on at least two phones
  • Satellite messenger for the party
  • Battery bank and cable
  • 3,000 to 4,000 calories, electrolytes, salty food
  • Leukotape, ibuprofen, basic first aid, trash bag

Layers and sun

  • Light fleece or hoody for the dark start
  • Wind shell, worn most of the day up high
  • Light insulated jacket for the pass and any stop
  • Rain shell, thin gloves, beanie
  • Sun hat, sunglasses, high-SPF sunscreen and lip balm
  • Sturdy shoes with grip on granite (trail runners are common)

Season-dependent

  • Microspikes and short gaiters when reports show snow on the pass or in the core
  • Dry spare socks in a bag
  • Bug head net in July
  • An ice axe means the pass is holding continuous snow, and the day is a different objective

Leave behind bear spray and anything packed "just in case" beyond the night-out layers. Weight on the descent costs more than it saves.

09 / Descent

The Snow Lakes descent

Viviane to Snow Lakes
Slab and rebar

Polished granite beside Snow Creek with a short rebar step. Stow poles, face in where needed, and keep the party together. Wet slab is slicker than the snow was.

Dam to Nada Lake
Last water

Fill everything. The lower trail is dry, exposed, and can be very hot. Sit down, eat, and put dry socks on if you have them before the long lower section.

Nada to the road
3,600 ft of switchbacks

Sandy switchbacks with little shade, and the section where fatigue and cramps usually show up. Take short scheduled stops rather than long ones. Near the bottom stay left on the trail toward the lot. Plan a rested driver, or a night in Leavenworth.
