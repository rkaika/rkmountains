Baseline

NWS first

Use the NWS hourly data here as the official, human-edited baseline. For raw model maps, compare Windy layers at the same time, layer, and altitude.

Close-in

HRRR inside 48 hours

For an alpine start, HRRR is usually the lead model for shower timing, wind shifts, and cloud detail once you are within about two days.

Cross-check

Globals + mesoscale

ECMWF is usually most accurate for 3–7 day synoptic timing; GEM often sees Pacific systems first; ICON and GFS add independent votes. If HRRR/NAM and the globals agree, confidence is high; if they diverge close-in, trust the mesoscale model.

Verdict logic

Worst hour, plus gates

Wind, gust, and precip use the worst hour in your window against the thresholds. V2 adds thunderstorm, visibility, and rain-on-snow gates that can only lower the verdict, and an avalanche gate in season. The timing strip shows when each problem lands.

Coordinates are precomputed route references, not official navigation data. NWS grid terrain is smoothed, so summit ridges can be colder, windier, or cloudier than the forecast grid cell. Snow level uses the freezing level minus roughly 1,000 ft.
